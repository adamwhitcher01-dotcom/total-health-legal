Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 October 2026

Total Health is a health app, so almost everything in it is sensitive. This policy says plainly what the app stores, what leaves your phone, and who else ever sees it. It covers the Total Health mobile app, published by AW LLC ("we", "us").

The short version: your logs live on your phone. They leave it only for things you switch on yourself, and they are never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared with anyone who isn't listed below.

What the app stores on your phone

Everything you log: weight and body fat, food and calories, symptoms and severity, medications and supplements, sleep, workouts and sets, runs and distances, energy check-ins, notes, appointments and your profile (name, birth date, height, sex, conditions you told us about).

This is stored in the app's own database on the device. If you uninstall the app without a backup or an account, it is gone - we cannot recover it.

What leaves your phone

Nothing leaves your phone unless one of these is on.

Your account (optional). If you sign in, your logs are copied to our database so you can use them on another phone and get them back if you lose this one. The database is hosted by Supabase. Rows are locked to your user id, so no other account can read them. We can technically access the database to run and fix the service, and we don't look at individual records for any other reason.

AI features (optional, paid). When you ask a question, the question and a summary of the relevant logs are sent through our server to Google's Gemini API, which generates the answer. The same happens when you ask it to estimate a food or import old entries. We don't keep the text of your questions or answers on our servers; the conversation is stored in your account like the rest of your data. Google processes the request under its API terms. AI features are off until you turn them on.

Health app connections (optional). If you connect Apple Health, Health Connect or Whoop, the app reads the types you approve (sleep, workouts, weight, body fat, steps, recovery) and stores them with your logs. On Android it can also write weight, body fat, exercise and nutrition back to Health Connect, so other apps you use stay in step. Data read from Apple Health is never used for advertising and is never sold or shared. You can disconnect at any time, and revoke access in the OS settings.

Subscriptions. Purchases are handled by Apple or Google. We use RevenueCat to tell whether your subscription is active. They receive a purchase identifier tied to your account id - never your health data.

Crash reports (optional, on by default). If the app crashes, a report is sent to Sentry so we can fix it: the error, where in the code it happened, the app version and the OS version. The reporter is configured to leave out who you are and what you logged, and you can turn it off in You → Backup and export → Privacy.

What we never do

We don't sell your data. We don't share it with advertisers or data brokers. There are no advertising SDKs, no third-party analytics and no tracking of you across other apps or websites. We don't use your health data to train AI models.

How long we keep it

Data in your account stays until you delete it. Deleting an entry in the app deletes it everywhere your account syncs. "Delete account" in You → Account removes your account and everything in it from our database; this cannot be undone. Anything stored only on your phone is deleted when you uninstall the app.

You can export everything you have logged at any time from You → Backup and export, as a file you keep.

Children

Total Health is not intended for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child has given us data, contact us and we will delete it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see the data we hold about you, correct it, export it, or have it deleted. The app does export and deletion directly; for anything else, write to us and we will answer within 30 days.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the app will say so before the change takes effect. The date at the top always reflects the current version.
